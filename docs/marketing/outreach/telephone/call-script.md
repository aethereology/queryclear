# Phone script — calling the outreach list

For calling the 146 businesses in `call-list.csv`. Read `README.md` first.

Three openers below, one per tier — **A** opened their report, **B** got emails and never
opened, **C** never received anything because their address bounced. Use the right one; the
tier is the second column of every row.

**The one thing that makes this work (tiers A and B):** these people already got an email
from you that ended with a single question — *"is being found in AI answers something
[business] is already thinking about, or not on the radar yet?"* — and **not one of them
answered it.** You are not cold-calling. You are asking a question you already asked in
writing, out loud, because email didn't work. That is a true, easy thing to say, and it's
the spine of every opener below.

---

## Before you dial (60 seconds per call, non-negotiable)

Open the row. Have on screen:

1. **`report_url`** — their actual report. You will reference it and possibly send them
   back to it live.
2. **`their_question_1`** — one real question their customers ask. Say it out loud once
   before dialing so it lands naturally.
3. **`technical_finding`** — a measured fact about their site, on 87 of the 146 rows. This
   is your strongest opener; use it whenever it's there. Two are listed where we found two —
   the second is your backup if they shrug the first off.
4. **`google_rating` / `google_reviews`** — a 4.9 with 200+ reviews means they already care
   about being found. Say so; it's a genuine compliment and it frames the whole call.

**Call in blocks by city.** All 48 Fort Lauderdale in one sitting, then the 47 in
Jacksonville. You get sharper, and "I've been working with businesses around Fort Lauderdale"
stays true.

**One warning:** `dermani MEDSPA` appears on five rows sharing one corporate number,
**(770) 884-4934**. It's a franchise — call it **once**, and pitch it as one conversation
covering five Atlanta locations. Dialing it five times is how you get hung up on.

**Best windows:** med spas and dental — Tue–Thu, 10–11:30am or 2–4pm. Avoid Monday
morning and lunch. Builders — early, 7:30–9am, before they're on site.

## Tier A opener — they opened the report (20 people)

These read their automated readiness preview. Lead with that. Don't pretend you don't know.

> "Hi, is this [first name / the owner]?
>
> Kyle here — I'm the one who sent over that website-readiness preview for [business] a couple weeks
> back. I saw you'd had a look at it, and I never followed up properly, so I figured I'd
> just call. **Did any of it actually land, or was it not really your problem?**"

Then **stop talking.** The question does the work.

Why it works: it's true, it gives them an easy out (which makes people talk), and "I saw
you'd had a look" is a fact you can state plainly — the report page records views.

**If they don't remember it:**
> "No problem at all — it was a short automated review of the public-site signals that
> search and AI systems can parse. It did not query ChatGPT live. Want me to give you the
> 30-second version, or should I just send the link again?"

## Tier B opener — emailed, never opened (122 people)

> "Hi, is this [first name / the owner]?
>
> Kyle here — I run queryclear, we're a small shop in Florida. I sent you an email a couple
> weeks ago that I'm guessing went straight past you, which is fair. It ended with one
> question and I never got an answer, so I'm just going to ask it out loud:
>
> **When someone asks ChatGPT or Google's AI 'best [their service] in [their city]' — do you
> know whether [business] comes up, or is that not on the radar yet?**"

Almost nobody knows. "No idea" is the answer you want — it's permission to keep going.

**Their likely responses:**

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "No idea." | "That's the honest answer most people give. Want to know what I found when I looked at your site?" |
| "Not on the radar." | "Fair enough. Can I give you 30 seconds on why I think it's worth being on it, and then you tell me to go away?" |
| "We already do SEO." | Go to **Objection 2**. |
| "What's this about?" | "Modern search readiness — I ran an automated review of your public site and found a few concrete clarity and technical gaps. It was not a live ChatGPT test. Two minutes?" |

## Tier C opener — the email bounced (4 people)

These four never received anything. Their address was dead. You have no shared history to
reference — but you have something better: a completely honest reason for calling, and a
small favour to ask, which is disarming.

> "Hi, is this [first name / the owner]?
>
> Kyle here — I run queryclear, small shop here in Florida. Slightly odd reason for calling:
> I tried to email [business] a couple of weeks ago and it **bounced straight back**, so the
> address on your site or listing may be dead — which you'd probably want to know, because if
> it bounced for me it's bouncing for customers too.
>
> That's genuinely worth two minutes on its own. And while I've got you — the reason I was
> emailing is I'd run an automated readiness check on the public pages search and AI systems
> can use as source material. It was not a live ChatGPT test. Want the short version?"

Lead with the bounce. It's a real problem you found, you're telling them for free, and it
costs them nothing to hear. It also proves you actually looked at their business.

**Which address bounced?** It's in the `email` column of their row. Have it ready — they will
ask, and reading it back to them is the moment they believe you.

## The 30 seconds (use only after they've said yes)

Plain, specific, no jargon:

> "For a question like '[their_question_1]', search and AI products may use indexes,
> retrieval, and public web pages as source material. Clear, specific pages give those
> systems more explicit facts to work from.
>
> When I looked at [their domain], **[the concrete finding]**. Which means for a question
> like that one, your site provides less explicit source material than it could.
>
> It's not a ranking thing, and it's not complicated to fix. Mostly it's how the pages are
> structured and labelled so the machine can tell what you do, where, and for who."

**Fill "[the concrete finding]" with, in order of preference:**
1. The `technical_finding` — *"your homepage is missing its page title entirely, which is
   the first thing any of these tools read"* (measured — say it flat).
2. Something you saw on their site in your 60 seconds of prep — thin service pages, no FAQ,
   treatments buried in an image, no city named anywhere.
3. The general read: *"your services are there for a human reading the page, but they're not
   labelled in a way these tools can parse."*

## The proof move (optional, powerful, use with care)

> "Do you have ChatGPT open? Ask it '[their_question_1]' and tell me what names come back."

**If they're not in the answer** — don't gloat. One beat, then:
> "That is one live sample, so it does not prove a ranking or explain why those names were
> selected. It does give us a dated baseline we can record alongside the site findings."

**If they ARE in the answer** — say so cheerfully. You don't lose:
> "Nice — that's genuinely good. That's one question out of the dozens people ask before
> they book. What I mapped for you was [q2] and [q3] as well. Worth knowing which ones
> you're winning and which ones you're not, rather than guessing."

**Only do this when you can watch it live with them.** Never claim a result you haven't
seen — see the guardrails below.

## Discovery — four questions, then shut up

Once they're talking, stop pitching. You need these four:

1. **"How do people find you today — mostly referrals, Google, Instagram?"**
   *(tells you if search matters to them at all)*
2. **"Who looks after the website — you, someone in-house, or an agency?"**
   *(the real decision path; "my nephew built it" and "we pay an agency $2k/mo" are
   completely different conversations)*
3. **"When did it last get properly worked on?"**
   *(3+ years = Upgrade. "We just rebuilt it" = Audit only.)*
4. **"If someone did find you through ChatGPT tomorrow, what would you want them to do —
   call, book online, fill in a form?"**
   *(gets them picturing the outcome, and tells you if the site can even convert)*

Write the answers in `call-log.csv`. They're worth more than the call outcome.

## The ask

**Default close — the walkthrough.** Not a sale. A booked 20 minutes.

> "Here's what I'd suggest. I've already got the report — nothing more to run. Give me
> twenty minutes on a screen share and I'll walk you through what I found, in your language,
> and tell you what I'd fix first and what I'd ignore. No charge, and if the answer is
> 'this isn't worth it for us,' I'll tell you that.
>
> Are mornings or afternoons better for you?"

Then **assume and offer two slots**: *"Thursday 10 or Thursday 2?"* Never "does that work?"

**Confirm out loud:** their email, the day, the time, and *"I'll send a calendar invite in
the next five minutes."* Then actually send it before your next call.

**If they're hot** — asking what it costs, how long, can you fix it — go straight there:

> "Two ways in. There's a paid audit at **$497** — a scored report I walk you through live,
> with a prioritised roadmap. If you decide to go ahead with the work, **that $497 comes
> straight off the build**, so it costs you nothing extra to start there.
>
> The work itself — rebuilding your service pages, metadata, schema, FAQs, the crawlability
> side — starts at **$2,500** for improving the site you've got, and **$6,500** if it needs a
> full rebuild. And if you want it looked after monthly after that, there's a care plan at
> **$997 a month** — cancel anytime, no contract.
>
> But I'd honestly rather do the free twenty minutes first so we both know which one you
> actually need."

Sending them to the paid audit when they need the free walkthrough loses the deal. Sending
them to the free walkthrough when they're ready to buy is never a mistake.

## Gatekeeper

The person answering a med spa or dental phone is booking appointments. Be brief, be warm,
be a person — never sound like a pitch.

> "Hi — I'm not a patient, I'll be quick. My name's Kyle, I sent [owner name] an email a
> couple weeks ago about their website. Is she around, or is there a better time to catch
> her?"

- **"What's it regarding?"** → *"Their website — how it shows up when people ask AI
  assistants for recommendations. I sent a report over; just following up on it."*
- **"Send an email."** → *"I did, that's the problem — it's probably in her spam. Is there a
  better time of day to catch her for two minutes?"*
- **"She's with a patient."** → *"Of course. When's she usually free — early morning?"*
  Then **call back when they said.** Mention it: *"Sarah said to try around now."*

Get the owner's **name** even if you don't get the owner. That alone makes the next call
twice as good — log it.

Say your number **twice**, slowly. Don't describe the problem in a voicemail — you'll talk

## Objections

**1. "How much does it cost?" (asked early, before value)**
> "Depends what you need, and I don't know that yet. The range is $2,500 for improving the
> site you have up to $6,500 for a full rebuild. The twenty-minute walkthrough is free, and
> that's what tells us which end you're at — or whether you need anything at all."

**2. "We already have an SEO guy / agency."**
> "Good — then most of the groundwork is probably there. What I'd ask is whether they've
> looked at the AI side specifically: schema markup, whether your pages answer questions
> directly, whether an assistant can tell what you do and where from the markup alone. Some
> agencies are on it. A lot are still working the 2019 playbook. I'll tell you honestly which
> yours is, and if they're doing it well I'll say so and get out of your way."

**3. "We're too busy / fully booked."**
> "Then this is genuinely the wrong month for you and I'll leave it. Can I check back in the
> autumn? …Out of interest, though — fully booked with the patients you want, or the ones
> you get?"

**4. "Just send me something."**
> "Happy to — you've already got it, actually. Let me resend the report link so it's at the
> top of your inbox. Can I do one thing though: **can I put twenty minutes in the diary for
> next week?** If the report answers everything you can cancel it and I won't chase you."
>
> *Never accept "send me something" as the end. It's a polite no. Trade it for a slot.*

**5. "Does this actually work? Can you guarantee we'll show up in ChatGPT?"**
> "No, and anyone telling you they can is lying to you. Nobody controls what ChatGPT says —
> not me, not Google, not OpenAI. What I can do is fix observable site-level gaps:
> unclear structure, missing markup, and missing answers on the page. That's readiness, not a
> ranking promise. If you want someone to promise you position one, I'm the wrong call."
>
> *This objection is a gift. Answering it this way is the most credible thing you'll say all
> call. **Never soften it.***

**6. "Who are you / never heard of you / how long have you been around?"**
> "Fair question. queryclear is a SparkCreatives Inc. brand, I'm the founder, and we're new —
> that's why I'm calling you myself instead of a salesperson, and why the audit's free. I'll
> show you exactly what I found and you can judge the work rather than the logo."
>
> *Don't invent clients, case studies, or years in business. New is fine. Fake is fatal.*

**7. "AI search isn't a real thing / nobody uses it."**
> "It might not be for your customers yet — worth checking rather than assuming. The thing
> is, most of what fixes AI readability is the same work that makes a site clearer for people
> and for Google anyway. You're not betting on a trend, you're getting a site that explains
> itself properly. The AI part is upside."

**8. "I don't have time right now."**
> "Understood — are you the right person to talk to about the website at all? …Then when's
> better, later this week or next? I'll call you then." *(Pin a time. "Call me sometime"
> is a no.)*

**9. "How did you get my number?"**
> "Your public Google listing — same place your customers find you. I emailed first, this is
> the follow-up. If you'd rather I didn't call again just say so and you're off my list for
> good."
>
> *If they ask to be removed: do it immediately, thank them, hang up. Then mark them
> `unsubscribed` (see README).*

**10. "Send it to my web guy."**
> "Perfect — what's his name and email? I'll send it over. **And can I stay on the thread?**
> Not to sell him anything; he'll have questions about the schema side and I'd rather answer
> them than have him guess."

## What you may not say — hard guardrails

The audit data has limits. Crossing them turns an honest pitch into a false claim, and
being the honest one is the entire position.

**Never say:**
- ❌ *"I ran your business through ChatGPT and you didn't come up."* — For nearly every row
  the citation check was **not measured** (`samples: 1`, `measured: false`). You did not
  verify this. **Do not say it.**
- ❌ *"You're invisible in AI search."* — Unmeasured. Also just untrue as an absolute.
- ❌ *"We'll get you cited in ChatGPT / ranked #1."* — Guaranteeing outcomes. Never.
- ❌ *"We work with a lot of med spas in your area."* — You have no med spa clients yet.
- ❌ Any invented client, testimonial, review, result, or number of years in business.

**Instead say:**
- ✅ *"For a question like '[q1]', your public site could provide clearer source material."*
  — This is a readiness claim supported by the page review, not a claim about a live answer.
- ✅ *"These are the questions I mapped for your business."* — Accurate: they were mapped
  from their services.
- ✅ *"Your homepage is missing its page title."* — Only when `technical_finding` says so.
  That one **is** measured. State it flat.
- ✅ *"I can't promise what ChatGPT will say. I can fix the site-level gaps we documented."*

**If they ask "did you actually check ChatGPT for my business?"** — tell the truth:
> "Not query by query, no. What I did was inspect the public pages and map representative
> buyer questions. Checking named products with a dated, labeled method is part of the paid audit —
> and honestly, we can check one right now together if you want."

That answer builds more trust than the claim you were tempted to make.

## After every call

1. **Log it** in `call-log.csv` — outcome, objection, next step, follow-up date.
2. **Had a real conversation?** Stop the emails immediately (README, rule 2). Do it before
   your next call or you'll forget.
3. **Booked something?** Send the invite within five minutes, while you're still fresh in
   their mind. Reference one specific thing they said.
4. **Asked to be removed?** Mark `unsubscribed`. Same day.

---

## Rhythm and what "good" looks like

**Per session:** 25 dials in a 90-minute block. Two blocks a day is 50 dials. The full list
is ~3 days of real dialing, or 2 weeks at a gentler pace.

**Rough expectations for cold-ish B2B follow-up calls:**

| | Tier A (20) | Tier B (122) | Tier C (4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach a decision-maker | 8–12 | 25–40 | 2–3 |
| Real conversation | 5–8 | 12–25 | 2–3 |
| Booked walkthrough | 2–4 | 5–12 | 1–2 |

Tier C punches above its size — a dead email address is a real problem they'll thank you for
finding, so the conversion rate per dial should be the highest on the list. Only four calls.
Make them early while you're fresh.

**One booked walkthrough that turns into a $2,500 Upgrade pays for every call on this list.**

**Two hard rules:**
- **Do not stop at 10 calls.** The first five are bad — everyone's first five are bad. The
  script starts working around call eight.
- **Rewrite this file after 30 calls.** Whatever objection shows up most in `call-log.csv`
  goes into the 30-second pitch so it never gets raised again. This script is a draft until
  real calls have edited it.
